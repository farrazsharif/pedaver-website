Pedaver / PQNK — Knowledge Outreach & AI Discoverability Audit

Ask PQNK's automated backend is paused. This audit evaluates whether pedaver.com — as it stands today, live in production — gives conventional search engines and AI retrieval systems the greatest legitimate opportunity to find, understand, and cite PQNK's agricultural knowledge. Every finding below is grounded in the live site (fetched directly, not inferred from source), the current codebase, current official crawler/search-engine documentation, and a real 50-query search test.

Executive audit

The technical foundation is genuinely stronger than typical for a site this size. The gap is not crawlability — it's that the site's most important discovery asset, real farmer questions, does not yet exist as published content.

Already strong

  • robots.txt is fully open — no crawler is blocked, no accidental noindex found
  • A real, linked JSON-LD entity graph: Organization ⇄ Person (founder), consistently reused across most page types
  • A sophisticated, taxonomy-driven internal-linking engine (fail-fast validated) — a genuine architectural asset
  • Clean canonical URLs, good titles/descriptions, consistent Open Graph — verified live
  • GA4 and Microsoft Clarity are live and collecting data today
  • An extensive, genuinely authored (not thin/scraped) knowledge corpus

What's actually blocking outreach

  • The Farmer Advisory page — the site's stated highest-priority discovery asset — is a content-empty placeholder: "0 notes" in all 5 categories, live, today
  • The 186-paper corpus was structured around PQNK's own vocabulary, not ordinary farmer phrasing — so even a fully crawlable page rarely matches how a real question is typed
  • Google Search Console verification appears broken (code is correct; the deploy secret is likely unset) — this blinds the primary measurement tool
  • No deliberate AI-crawler policy exists — training and citation bots are currently allowed identically, by default, not by decision
  • Structured-data coverage has real but fixable inconsistencies (detailed in §7–§8)
The central finding: nothing technical is preventing Pedaver.com from being crawled, indexed, or cited. What's missing is the bridge between how PQNK explains its science and how a farmer actually asks about it — and the one page purpose-built to be that bridge currently has no content on it.

Current crawl/index status

Verified against the live production site, not inferred from source.

robots.txt — fetched live from pedaver.com/robots.txt
User-Agent: * Allow: / Sitemap: https://pedaver.com/sitemap.xml

Matches src/app/robots.ts exactly — no drift between deployed and current source. Fully open; no crawler-specific rules exist at all (see §3).

sitemap.xml — fetched live
  • 271 URLs, single flat <urlset> (not a sitemap index — fine at this scale).
  • Generated by src/app/sitemap.ts: a hardcoded staticPages array plus a map over the crops/papers/machines/resources content arrays.
  • Every entry has <lastmod>, but the semantics are inconsistent: static/crop/machine/resource pages get the build's current timestamp (new Date() at build time — technically "last build," not "last content change"); paper pages get paper.publishedDate directly.
  • Confirmed bug, but a nuanced one: one entry (technical-adaptations-for-mechanized-sri-production) shows lastmod: 2011-01-09. This is not bad data — the paper's publishedDate correctly records its real external publication (Springer, Paddy and Water Environment 9(1):111–119, 2011; externalUrl and externalPublisher fields confirm this is an externally-cited paper, not Pedaver's own authored content). The bug is that sitemap.ts uses this field for lastmod regardless of whether it's "when Pedaver last touched this page" or "when the cited external work was originally published" — those are different facts, and only the first belongs in a sitemap's lastmod.
  • No other implausible dates found in this pass (full corpus scan in §7).
Redirects & near-duplicates

docs/htaccess.txt defines exactly two 301s: /farmer-stories/ → /videos/ and /certification/ → /validation/. /videos and /video-library are not duplicate content (confirmed: zero overlapping YouTube video IDs between their content sources) — but /videos is absent from the main header navigation, reachable only via the sitemap and a single contextual link on crop pages. Not a duplicate-content risk; a discoverability/crawl-depth one.

No hard indexing blocker found — no accidental noindex, no broken canonical, no orphaned robots directive.

IndexNow, confirmed via the current spec at indexnow.org: a simple ping (HTTP GET or batched JSON POST, up to 10,000 URLs) telling participating engines a URL was added, updated, or deleted — a real-time push instead of waiting for organic re-crawl. Current participants: Bing, Yandex, Naver, Seznam.cz, Yep — Google does not participate.

Should IndexNow fire automatically on publish? Yes. The natural hook is the GitHub Actions deploy workflow (.github/workflows/deploy.yml) that already runs on every push: a build step could diff the newly-changed content-derived URLs (papers/science/advisory) against the previous deploy and ping IndexNow with just those, at effectively zero ongoing cost or manual effort. Not built in this audit — a concrete, scoped P3 item (§15).

ChatGPT Search discoverability

OAI-SearchBot is not blocked — the wide-open robots.txt allows it, so nothing currently prevents pedaver.com pages from being discovered, retrieved, summarized, or cited by ChatGPT Search. This is confirmed against OpenAI's own current crawler documentation, not assumed.

The same wildcard rule also allows GPTBot (training) and ChatGPT-User (live user-initiated fetch) identically — see §3 for why this is a decision point, not a defect.

Beyond the robots.txt question, ChatGPT Search's actual citation behavior depends on the same fundamentals as conventional search: is the page indexed/crawlable (yes), does it contain clear, well-structured, genuinely informative content that answers the query (uneven — strong on Science/Paper pages, absent on Farmer Advisory), and can the retrieval system confidently attribute the content to an authoritative source (helped by the Organization/Person entity graph in §8–§9). No OpenAI documentation was found describing any additional, ChatGPT-Search-specific technical requirement beyond being crawlable and genuinely useful.

Knowledge Paper machine-comprehension

Programmatic, corpus-wide inspection of all 186 papers (confirmed count — src/lib/content/papers.ts and src/lib/content/knowledge/metadata.json agree exactly). Systematic findings, not a per-paper list. One correction to note up front: the discoverability metadata (crops, field problems, questions answered, science domains, authority status, science links) does not live in papers.ts itself — it lives in a separate, parallel metadata.json, keyed by slug.

The single most important finding in this section: 1,027 real, farmer-phrased questions already exist in the data — and are never shown to a crawler or an AI system. Every one of the 186 papers has a questionsAnswered field (3–8 entries each, zero papers with none) — genuine practical questions written in plain language. But src/app/papers/[slug]/page.tsx never renders this field on the page, and the paper's JSON-LD never includes it either. The only place these questions are used at all is client-side search ranking (search.ts) and the internal relatedness scoring (related.ts) — both invisible to anything reading the page's actual content, whether a search crawler or an AI retrieval system. This is a direct, evidenced explanation for several of the 50-query benchmark misses (§14): the answer to "why does mulch suffocate roots" may already be written as one of these questions, on an already-published, already-indexed page — just never displayed.

A governance nuance worth carrying into §9: 54 papers (29% of the corpus) are explicitly marked "Requires PQNK Review" — not yet approved current knowledge. A machine reading raw content without respecting this field would treat provisional material as equally authoritative as approved knowledge. This is a legitimate, working governance signal already in the data — it just needs to keep being respected as any new surfacing of this content (e.g. rendering questionsAnswered, per above) gets built.

Two related gaps found in how the data that does exist gets used: the paper detail page's ScholarlyArticle JSON-LD (§8) emits only headline, description, author, publisher, url, image — omitting keywords, about/mentions (which could carry the already-tagged scientificDomains/crops), and citation, despite all of this data already existing in metadata.json. Separately, src/lib/content/crops.ts's crop→paper linking (crossLinks.ts) matches on paper titles containing the crop's name, rather than using the already-structured crops field in metadata.json — a strictly better join key that's already there and already used elsewhere in the codebase, just not here.

Internal-link architecture

Two taxonomy-driven functions in src/lib/content/knowledge/related.ts power all cross-linking between Papers and Science pages — no manual link lists beyond 3 hand-identified companion pairs:

  • Paper → related Papers (used on every paper detail page): weighted overlap scoring — same field problem (highest weight) → same crop → same practice → same Science domain → same production stage → same machinery → shared PQNK principle (tiebreaker) — plus explicit companion pairs that always win.
  • Science → related Papers (used on all 10 Science pages): one hand-curated "anchor" paper per domain, chosen by a human reviewer with documented reasoning per domain, plus algorithmically-selected supporting papers with a diversity pass (prefers introducing a new crop/problem over repeating one already shown). The anchor selection is fail-fast validated — the build throws if a configured anchor slug is ever removed or de-approved, rather than silently falling back to a wrong pick.
This is a real architectural strength, not boilerplate — it's specifically designed to avoid the "breadth mistaken for relevance" failure mode (a broad multi-topic paper outranking a precisely on-topic one), a lesson visibly baked into the scoring weights and the anchor-curation rationale.

Coverage gap: this engine only operates on Papers and Science pages. Crops, Machines, and (once populated) Farmer Advisory are not part of the same relationship graph — see §9.

Structural vs. visible linking: the relationship data computed by this engine renders as real <a> links on the page (good for both crawling and human navigation) but is not also expressed as structured data (e.g. no relatedLink/about properties in the JSON-LD) — a machine currently has to infer the relationship from link position/anchor text rather than read it as an explicit fact.

Farmer-question discoverability

The single most important finding in this audit.

Live, today: every one of the Farmer Advisory page's 5 categories reads "0 notes." Verified directly from production HTML — Adoption Difficulties, Planting, Water Management, Symptoms & Diagnosis, and General Questions each display: "This library is being built from real questions and voice notes shared across Pedaver's PQNK WhatsApp groups. Check back as new notes are added." None of the real farmer questions named in this audit's brief — water not infiltrating, yellowing after rain, weeds through mulch, hardpan, the 4 kg NP transition rate, mulch suffocating roots — currently exist anywhere on the live site in question-and-answer form.

This directly explains a visible pattern in the 50-query benchmark (§14): queries phrased in exactly this kind of ordinary language returned zero Pedaver presence, even on topics — hardpan, mulch mechanics — where PQNK's own technical explanation is genuinely distinctive and already documented in the Science/Paper corpus. The knowledge exists; the question-shaped bridge to it doesn't.

The cheapest, fastest win in this entire audit: a bridge already exists and is already written. §7 found that all 186 Knowledge Papers already carry a questionsAnswered field — 1,027 real, plain-language questions in total — that is never rendered on the page and never included in structured data. Before writing a single new sentence, simply displaying this already-authored content (as a visible "Questions this paper answers" section, and in the paper's JSON-LD as keywords or within the description) closes a meaningful part of the gap at near-zero editorial cost and zero new-content risk. This should happen first, ahead of authoring anything new.
Recommended approach — content, not markup

Since Google has retired FAQ rich results entirely (§4) and Ask PQNK's automated Q&A system is paused, every lever here is purely editorial/rendering work, fully within this audit's "no automated farmer-answering" constraint:

  1. Render the existing 1,027 questionsAnswered entries on their paper pages (and surface them in JSON-LD) — no new writing required, this is exposure work on content that already exists (§7, §15 P1).
  2. Populate a real, modest first batch of Farmer Advisory entries using the actual WhatsApp-sourced questions named in this audit's brief — written as genuine content a human would read, in the farmer's own phrasing, answered by Pedaver directly. A first pass of 10–15 authentic entries against the existing category structure would already end the "0 notes" placeholder problem.
  3. Where a genuinely new question isn't already covered by an existing paper's questionsAnswered list, embed it as a natural subheading inside the relevant Science page or Knowledge Paper — e.g. the Water Science page gains a passage genuinely titled "Why is water not going into my soil?" This strengthens pages that are already authoritative rather than only adding new ones, with zero risk of reading as keyword-stuffed, since the questions are real and the answers are the site's own existing science.

All three are P1 items in §15 — deliberately scoped as content and rendering work, not a system to build. Item 1 should come first: it's the same fix in spirit as items 2–3, but the content is already written.

YouTube & Facebook integration strategy

The three authenticated Pedaver media sources (Facebook Pedaver, Pedaver PQNK YouTube, Aasif Sharif YouTube) are already correctly declared via sameAs in the global Organization JSON-LD, verified live — a real, existing strength that establishes the entity linkage without any further work.

/video-library (47 Urdu farmer lectures, organized by topic) and /videos (official + testimonial content) are both live, distinct, non-duplicate pages — but neither carries VideoObject structured data, and no evidence of transcripts or timestamped subject summaries was found for either.

Recommendation — selective, not exhaustive (per the explicit "don't ingest everything yet" instruction)
  • VideoObject markup (name, description, thumbnailUrl, uploadDate, embedUrl) for a deliberately small, high-value first set — videos already referenced from crop pages or named in Knowledge Papers, not all 47 lectures at once.
  • Contextual links from the relevant Knowledge Paper to the specific video (timestamped where the platform supports it), rather than a generic "watch our videos" link — this is what makes the website the canonical hub and YouTube the supporting evidence, matching the stated goal directly.
  • Dedicated website pages only for a handful of flagship videos (e.g. a major transition case study) where the content genuinely warrants standalone treatment — not a blanket policy of one page per video.
  • Full transcription/ingestion of the 47-lecture library is explicitly out of scope for this pass, per instruction — flagged as a P3/future decision, not proposed here.
